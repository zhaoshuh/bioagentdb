How to use BioAgentDB

A five-minute guide: search the catalog, read a record, ask the agent, mine a topic — and know why you can trust what you see.

1What this is

BioAgentDB is a catalog of datasets announced in biomedical papers — for any research topic (diseases are just one kind). For every record it keeps the paper's own words — a verbatim evidence quote — and a live link to the data. It never stores the data itself.

  • The AI agent reads each paper: abstract and Data Availability statement.
  • Three adversarial reviewers challenge every claim before it enters the catalog.
  • Every kept record carries the exact sentence it came from and a checked link.
  • Missing something? Workbench to mine it — Ming keeps a task running until the literature is exhausted, then patrols for new papers (step 5).
Records are read-only by design — nothing here can be edited or deleted from the interface.

2Search the catalog

  • Type into the search box: a topic ("Alzheimer", "gut microbiome"), a gene ("APOE"), a tissue, a modality, or an accession like GSE277709.
  • Combine with the filters: topic, modality, organism, and status.
  • Press Enter or the Search button. Results are paper cards, newest first.
Every result keeps its accessions and its evidence quote — you can judge a record without leaving the list.

3Read a record

  • Click any card to open the detail view.
  • See the datasets announced in the paper, each with a verified chip, repository, accession and confidence.
  • Follow the repository link (and direct download where available) to get the data.
  • Read the evidence: the zone-tagged sentence (abstract or Data Availability) that the record is grounded in.

4Workbench

  • Describe what you need in plain language — any research topic (a disease, a biological process or a technique) with organ/organism details ("single-nucleus RNA-seq of human brain in Alzheimer's disease, at least 20 samples").
  • The agent may ask one clarifying question (for example, the organism); answer it or say "any" to skip.
  • You get a report with two parts: catalog records — verified, with evidence, and papers to inspect (live-source finds that are not yet verified) — press Verify now to read a paper's full text and check its links on the spot.
  • Every search adds a result set to the RESULTS panel — timestamped, newest first. Expand a set to review its records (By paper or By dataset), and tick the sets you want to export.
  • Open any cited record right where you are — view record → shows its datasets, links and evidence in place — or tick result sets in the RESULTS panel and export them as JSONL / CSV.
Non-biomedical questions are refused on purpose — the agent only answers what the catalog and the papers can support.

5Mine anything — Ming

Missing something? Open Workbench and switch to Ming — the mining mode. Describe the topic in one sentence; the agent chats it through with you — plain-text follow-up questions, free-text answers (say any to skip) — and then proposes the task.

  • Chat — the agent keeps asking until it has enough; answer in your own words in the same composer.
  • Start Ming — a resident scheduler mines in the background: first until the literature is exhausted, then a patrol for new papers.
  • Verified records are published to the public catalog and also tracked per task (operators can set a private-only mode in the config file).
Deploying your own? With the default settings a fresh instance starts blank — you mine exactly the topics you need.

6Track runs — My mining

  • My mining lists your tasks: status, records found, papers read, and the original intent you asked for.
  • Pause / resume / stop / restart any task — a stopped or failed task goes back to the queue with Restart (it resumes when the scheduler is running); export its records as JSONL or CSV.
  • Topics (switch with the Tasks | Topics tabs): rename a topic, or hide it from the catalog lists — hiding is reversible, and a hidden topic reappears when you mine it again.
  • Each record is the same verified object you see in the public catalog.

7Export

  • In the ledger, press Export to download a CSV of the dataset records.
  • In the Ask workbench, each search is a result set in the RESULTS panel — tick the sets you want, then export them as JSONL or CSV.
  • Exports contain metadata, evidence quotes and links — never the data files themselves.

8Why you can trust it

  • T1 — grounded: a record's accession must appear in its own evidence quote, taken from the paper (a regrounding step finds the exact sentence if needed).
  • T2 — reachable: the link is checked; the record says when it was last verified.
  • Three adversarial reviewers — veracity, coverage, metadata — challenge every candidate before it is kept; rejected and fixed records stay in the audit trail.
  • Statuses: "verified" = passed both gates; "paper-verified" = grounded in the paper text with a checked link.
The result: you can always trace a record back to the sentence that announced it — and to a live link. That is the whole point.

9FAQ

Can I edit or delete a record?
No — read-only is a design rule. Records change only through a new mining release.
Where does the data live?
In the original repositories (GEO, PRIDE, dbGaP, Zenodo…). BioAgentDB keeps the announcement, the evidence and the link.
How fresh is the catalog?
This build is v1.6 (October 2026); the agent keeps reading new papers, so the catalog is designed to grow with the literature.
What does an accession look like?
GSE… (GEO), PXD… (PRIDE), phs… (dbGaP), E-MTAB… (BioStudies), 10.5281/zenodo… — search any of them directly.
Can I mine a topic that is not a disease?
Yes — diseases are just one kind of topic. Use Ming (Workbench → Ming) and describe whatever you need.
Where do mined records go?
By default they are verified and published to the public catalog, and also tracked per task under My mining. Operators can switch to a private-only mode in the config file.
Can I run my own instance?
Yes — run the Docker deployment; with the default settings it starts blank (no starter themes) and grows with exactly what you mine.
Can I cite this catalog?
Yes — cite the paper and the release version shown in the footer of the home page.
