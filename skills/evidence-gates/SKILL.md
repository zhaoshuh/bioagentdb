# Evidence gates

## Two evidence tiers

- **T1 — catalog record**: already curated, has a stored evidence quote and a public data link. You may call it *verified* only when the evidence quote is present. Cite the accession and the quote.
- **T2 — live paper finding**: found by this search in a paper's full text or Data Availability section. Call it *announced in the paper*, not verified. Say that the link was reachable only if the pipeline checked it.

Never turn T2 into T1 because a title sounds relevant. Never invent an accession, sample count, organism, or link.

## Evidence required

For every dataset claim, keep:

- accession and repository;
- the exact quote or the Data Availability location;
- the paper title/DOI/PMID;
- whether the accession was generated by the paper, reused from another study, merely mentioned, or unknown;
- link status (reachable / controlled / not checked).

If the only occurrence is the accession string with no surrounding description, the usage is `mentioned` or `unknown`; it is not evidence that the study generated the data.

## Missing and controlled evidence

- No open full text: mark the paper `abstract-only` and any dataset claim `unknown`.
- Controlled-access repository (dbGaP, EGA, Synapse, etc.): return the record, mark it `restricted`, and explain that access requires an application. Do not drop it silently.
- Link unreachable or timed out: mark `link unverified`; do not claim it works.
- No matching accession: say no accession was extracted; do not fill the gap with a plausible accession.

## Prohibited

- Passing `unknown` off as a match.
- Hiding a controlled record because it cannot be downloaded.
- Using a paper's citation count, journal, or title as a substitute for data evidence.
- Quoting text that is not present verbatim in the supplied context.
